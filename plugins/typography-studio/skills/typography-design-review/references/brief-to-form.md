# From a brief to a coherent drawing

## Define the system

Record use, tone, script/language, repertoire, sizes, platforms and references.
Describe proportions, curve behavior, stroke contrast, terminal logic, counter
shape and spacing. A family name or category is not a construction plan.

| Aim | Possible design question | Required evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Strong display | How much mass can the counters sustain? | Headlines at actual size, both polarities |
| Quiet long reading | What rhythm and recognition survive repeated lines? | Paragraphs, punctuation and small sizes |
| Compact interface | How do narrow widths affect recognition and marks? | Labels, data columns and zoom/reflow |
| Expressive identity | Which exceptions are deliberate and consistent? | The same gesture across relevant classes |

These are questions, not prescriptions. A round sans can be sober; an angular
letter can be caricatural. Locate a conflicting gesture in the actual word.
Distinguish stroke contrast, text/background contrast and hierarchy contrast.

## Build structure, black and white together

1. Establish the skeleton and proportions by classes. In Latin, H controls
   straight stems, O round forms, n stems/shoulders/joins; e and s test openings,
   transitions and terminal rhythm. Adapt the controls to another script.
2. Draw the exterior and counter together. Do not place a default centered hole
   inside an otherwise finished exterior.
3. Use purposeful curve transitions and extrema. Explain where curvature,
   tension or mass changes instead of calling every new curve "more refined".
4. Compensate perceived weight across stems, horizontals, curves and joins.
   Equal geometric thickness does not guarantee equal optical weight.
5. Relate cuts and terminals to the structure and neighboring letters.
6. Adjust sidebearings in groups, then word space and exceptional pairs.

Keep editable contours and a prior iteration. Render licensed reference fonts
for study, but do not trace them, extract their contours or interpolate them
into an original design without the required rights and honest attribution.

## Compare in context

Observe words at intended sizes before enlarged outlines. Align baselines
deliberately across reference families; the same CSS size does not imply the
same apparent height. Do not normalize width or use corrective tracking to
hide a shape mismatch in the comparison.

Expand from controls to words, marks, punctuation and complete repertoire.
Four good controls do not approve the future alphabet. Avoid silent fallback.

## Reject or continue for a concrete reason

- `REDRAW_REQUIRED`: a visible severe mismatch to the brief or the system.
- `FUNCTIONAL_CHECK_INCOMPLETE`: insufficient covered words/context to judge.
- `GATHER_EVIDENCE`: apparent issue depends on an untested rasterizer or size.
- `LOCALLY_ACCEPTABLE`: the observed corpus supports the intended function.

Record the gesture, its context, likely cause and uncertainty. A tangency fix
can be correct while the curve remains visually wrong. Do not average a severe
voice defect against successful technical checks.
