# Teaching influences

These notes are original summaries of educational ideas, with attribution.
They are not a current university syllabus, a qualification, or an endorsement.
The package does not redistribute the teaching PDFs or other authors' text.

## A coherent visual system

Pablo Cosgaya's 2016 Typography 1 program emphasizes sensitivity to form/color,
family constants/variables, construction through structure → stroke → counters
and endings, and reasoned formative critique. Apply that order before asking
an agent to produce an entire alphabet or optimize a contour numerically.

OERT articles by Marcela Romero and collaborators connect the individual sign
to the word, shared white spaces, line spacing, repetition and rhythm. Its
fundamental concepts treat the family and its variables as a system. Observe
these relationships at actual sizes, not only in a glyph atlas.

Eduardo Gabriel Pepe's *Diseño tipográfico e identidad* (Bold 2, 2015, pp. 56–66)
connects form, function, identity and an iterative project process. A reflection
by Rubén Fontana is encountered there as a secondary citation; do not attribute
unread passages or project approval to Fontana.

## Practical digital construction

The community's *Design With FontForge* covers trusting visual judgment,
o/n controls, optical spacing, word space and validation in use. Its advice
supports contextual correction, not equality of ink areas or a universal
geometric recipe.

Google Fonts' historical gf-docs material distinguishes editable sources,
reproducible builds, technical checks, diffs and real renders. The repository
is deprecated; consult its stated successor/current submission requirements
for a Google Fonts contribution rather than treating old checklists as policy.

## Convert study to practice

Write a construction brief, draw a small control set, render it in words,
inspect both black and white, record a prediction and compare a bounded change.
Then expand repertoire/family and revisit the full composition. Keep rejected
attempts and feedback in the user's project, not as a hardcoded font preference
inside this skill.
